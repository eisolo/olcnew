Other people's knives

Reviews

A few written reviews that have stuck to the line. Scores are theirs.

Everyday Commentary, September 2016, on the Wayfarer 247. "What an amazing knife. I was blown away the first time I fired it open." The written score was 19 out of 20. The summary line was "Pure, solid awesomeness." The review.

Everyday Commentary, December 2017, on the Busker. 18 out of 20. "Custom quality, production availability and design creativity. We have a winner." Design, fit, steel, and the lock were the high marks. The review.

BladeReviews, December 2018, on the 247. The knife they had was 8.125 inches overall, a 3.5 inch blade, 5 ounces, made in America. Their close: if you want a titanium flipper frame lock of that quality, and you want to make it yours, the 247 is an excellent choice. The review.

Knife Informer on the Swish. Blade 3.75 inches, overall 8.62, weight 4.6 ounces on their scale, Elmax, frame lock, flipper, USA. They scored quality and performance at 83, value at 77, overall 80, and called it mid-tech done right. The clip and the lockbar were the complaints. The review.

Video notes that match the shop's own history: early Wayfarer, Rainmaker, and Gambit customs had no CNC. From 2016, the parts that move were machined so the action would stay consistent, and the finish stayed in Visalia. Alexander Birykov is credited with the Rainmaker and the Swish. Serge Panchenko with the Gambit.
